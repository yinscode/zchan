How it works

One channel. Many purchases.

Open a channel once and every purchase after it is one signature. The chain sees the open and the settlement, and nothing in between. Try it below.

Each purchase adds its price plus the solver's 100 zat fee to one signed total.

Two ways in

For the agent that pays. For the service that gets paid.

Agents

Give an agent a budget, not a card.

  • One channel pays every merchant through a solver, with no account or API key per seller.
  • The channel's capacity is a spending cap the agent cannot exceed.
  • Before funding, the solver signs a refund, so unspent funds always come back.
  • Speaks x402 v2 and MPP, whichever the merchant offers.

Merchants and services

Charge per request without changing your API.

  • Proxy mode answers 402 in front of any HTTP service, with a price per path.
  • One channel from the solver carries every agent's payments to you.
  • Earnings arrive at a shielded address when the channel closes.
  • The same channels run QR checkout at a counter, with "Paid" in about a second.

Protocol

Five messages. No block to wait for.

One real payment from the hosted playground, timed from the agent's first request.

01Merchant

Offer. A request without payment gets 402 and a fresh invoice the merchant signed, offered over x402 and MPP.

1 ms
02Solver

Quote. The solver checks the invoice and its liquidity, then signs a quote: amount plus fee.

5 ms
03Agent

Sign. The agent signs its channel's next total, with a payment id that makes retries safe.

15 ms
04Solver → merchant

Forward. The solver raises its own channel to the merchant, and the merchant signs a receipt.

15 ms
05Agent → merchant

Fetch. The agent repeats the request with the receipt. The merchant serves it once.

17 ms

The solver sees the payment graph; Zchan does not hide it from the solver. Refunds and unilateral closes are transparent transactions and reveal the split. Merchants also see the client's network metadata, such as its IP address.

Security and status

What the chain enforces, and what it doesn't.

Enforced by the chain

  • An agent's unspent balance: a refund signed before funding, valid at the lock height.
  • What a merchant has acknowledged: it holds the signed state and can close with it.
  • Solver liquidity in merchant channels: the same presigned refund.

Trusted today

  • The solver forwarding a paid request: at most one payment in flight per agent.
  • The agent leg and the merchant leg are not atomic; hash-locked states are planned.
  • The solver sees which agent paid which merchant.

Status

  • Live on testnet: channels, solver, x402 and MPP, proxy mode, QR checkout, shielded funding and closes.
  • Next: TypeScript and Python clients, an MCP payment tool, USD prices.
  • Before mainnet: external review of channel code and key handling. Mainnet stays disabled in code until then.
