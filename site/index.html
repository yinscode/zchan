For AI agents and merchants

Pay per request. Settle once.

zchan gives every agent one Zcash payment channel and pays any merchant through it. Each payment is a signed channel state: sub-second and gasless, with no transaction and no network fee. The chain sees the channel open and the channel close, and nothing in between.

Benefits

Micropayments that make sense on chain

A Zcash transaction costs at least 10,000 zatoshis and waits for a block. A channel spreads that cost over every payment it carries, and payments clear as fast as an HTTP round trip.

8–35 ms · 0 network fee per payment

Sub-second and gasless

Each payment raises the channel's cumulative level by one signature: no transaction, no block, no network fee. Only the open and the close touch the chain, so a 100-zatoshi call is worth charging for. (Times measured on testnet with agent, solver and merchant on one host; add your network's round trips.)

refund signed before funding

Funds always come back

Before an agent funds a channel, the other side signs a time-locked refund. If anyone disappears, the agent's watcher broadcasts it at the lock height.

x402 v2 · MPP

Paid in plain HTTP

Merchants answer 402 Payment Required with a signed invoice, offered over both x402 and MPP. The client pays and retries with the merchant's own signed receipt.

1 agent channel → every merchant

One channel, every merchant

A solver keeps one channel to each merchant and nets the agents' payments through it. Agents never open a channel per seller.

Ironwood funding · Ironwood close

Shielded in, shielded out

Fund a channel from shielded notes and close it with both sides paid to Ironwood. The chain sees the channel's size and when it opened and closed, not where the money came from or how it was split.

--upstream · --routes

Gate any API, unchanged

Proxy mode puts an existing service behind payment. Set a price per path; zchan answers 402, checks payment, and forwards the request.

How a payment works

Five messages, no block to wait for

Merchant

Offer

A request without payment gets 402 and a fresh invoice signed by the merchant.

Solver

Quote

The solver checks the invoice and its liquidity, then signs a quote: amount plus fee.

Agent

Sign

The agent signs its channel's next cumulative state and sends it with a payment id that makes retries safe.

Solver → merchant

Forward

The solver raises its own channel to the merchant by the invoice amount. The merchant signs an acknowledgment.

Agent → merchant

Fetch

The agent repeats the request with the acknowledgment. The merchant serves it once.

Agentic use cases

Agents that buy what they need, call by call

An agent with a funded channel can pay any zchan merchant without an account, an API key or a card on file. Its exposure is capped by the channel's capacity.

x402 · MPP~100–5,000 zat a call

Pay-per-call APIs

Search, market data, geocoding, inference. The provider prices each path, and the agent pays only for the calls it makes.

Why channels: per-call prices are far below one on-chain fee.

MCPper tool call

Paid tools for assistants

MCP servers charge per tool invocation, so a specialized tool can be monetized without subscriptions or key management.

Why channels: one agent channel pays every tool server through the solver.

budgetscapacity = limit

Data procurement with a budget

A research agent buys articles, datasets and reports from many sources within a fixed spend.

Why channels: the channel's capacity is a hard spending cap the agent cannot exceed.

agent ↔ agentservice fees

Agents paying agents

A planning agent hires specialist agents (translation, code review, image work) and pays each per task.

Why channels: a new counterparty needs no new channel, only a solver that pays it.

meteredper second, per MB

Compute and storage metering

GPU minutes, sandbox time and storage are billed as they are used rather than in prepaid blocks.

Why channels: states update as often as the meter ticks, at no chain cost.

shielded payoutIronwood

Private revenue for providers

Providers receive their day's earnings at a shielded address when the channel closes, and the payer's change goes back shielded too.

Why channels: one close with no transparent outputs, instead of a public trail of every sale.

Real-time commerce

Checkout at the speed of a tap

The same channels work at a counter. A merchant shows a QR code that carries only an invoice reference, and the buyer approves on their phone. The merchant sees "Paid" in about a second, without waiting for a block.

QR checkout≈1 s to paid

Counters and cafés

The POS shows the amount and a QR code. The buyer's agent fetches and verifies the signed invoice, shows the merchant, amount and fee, and pays on approval.

Why channels: confirmation in one round trip instead of one block.

machinesper kWh, per minute

Charging, Wi-Fi, vending, parking

A machine meters usage and the buyer's channel advances as it runs. Stopping the session stops the payments.

Why channels: pay-as-you-go without prepaid credit or a card hold.

streamingper minute

Media and live events

Pay per minute watched, per article read or per tip sent during a stream, with no minimum top-up.

Why channels: payments small enough to match the content.

nettingdaily settlement

Suppliers and marketplaces

A marketplace operator acts as the solver, netting many buyers' payments into one channel per seller and settling daily.

Why channels: one transaction per seller per day, with every payment reconciled.

Developers

Put your API behind payment in one command

Proxy mode needs no code change in your service. Clients pay with x402 v2 or MPP, whichever they speak.

Prices per path, with prefix matches like /api/*
Payment headers stripped before your service sees the request
Each invoice served once; replays, tampered and mismatched credentials refused
An upstream error releases the invoice so the client can retry
Idempotent payments that survive a solver crash or a merchant outage
# Merchant: gate an existing API
zchan-merchant --upstream http://localhost:3000 \
  --routes '/api/search=3000,/api/*=500' \
  --solver https://solver.example --admin-token "$TOKEN"

# Agent: open a channel once
zchan-agent open --capacity 100000 --lock-blocks 2000

# Agent: pay and fetch over x402 v2 ...
zchan-agent get https://api.example/api/search?q=zec

# ... or over MPP, any method
zchan-agent get https://api.example/api/echo \
  --mpp --method POST --body 'hello'

Trust model

What the chain enforces, and what it doesn't

zchan states its guarantees plainly. Funds locked in channels are protected by the chain. The solver is trusted with one payment per agent at a time.
